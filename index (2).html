✦ A gentle little check-in

Find one little thing to hold onto.

Some days, gratitude comes easily. Other days, it can be harder to find. Looking in a few different places may help: a good moment, what helped it happen, a simple pleasure, another person, or something useful already around you.

These five questions are inspired by gratitude research and reflection exercises. Your score only guides the suggestions. It is not a scientific test of how grateful you are. For entertainment purposes only. ☺️

There are no wrong answers. Go with the flow and how you feel. Skip anything that doesn’t fit.
Where these prompts come from
  • The validated GQ-6 measures a person's general tendency to experience gratitude. This app does not reproduce or score that scale. Penn Positive Psychology Center.
  • Research on gratitude includes appreciation of other people, a sense of abundance, and noticing simple pleasures. Watkins et al. (2003).
  • Gratitude exercises have asked people to list things that went well and reflect on what caused them. Emmons & McCullough (2003); Seligman et al. (2005).
